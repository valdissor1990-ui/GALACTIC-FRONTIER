# Ship Art Direction — GALACTIC FRONTIER

## Current status

The original seven-class fleet is integrated into the Godot project. The current work is visual refinement and gameplay integration, not a claim of final AAA-quality art.

## Visual target

Target a polished 2018–2020 AAA-era real-time game presentation at Full HD (1920×1080). The goal is strong art direction, readable silhouettes and convincing materials—not 4K assets or maximum polygon counts:
- Distinct, readable silhouettes at gameplay distance and convincing construction when viewed close-up.
- Layered hard-surface forms: primary hull, secondary armor, recessed seams, access hatches, vents, heat exchangers, actuators, hardpoints and fasteners.
- Purposeful cockpit glazing and framing, readable engine architecture, weapon mounts and navigation/sensor hardware.
- PBR materials with controlled roughness/metalness variation, subtle micro-surface detail and restrained emissive elements.
- Lighting that reveals form without crushing the hull into black or clipping emissive surfaces into flat neon shapes.
- Ship armor uses procedural, object-space micro-normal and roughness breakup; keep the effect subtle enough that it reads as machined metal rather than noisy stone.
- The flight backdrop keeps its planet and nebula landmarks camera-relative across the full mission route, with a restrained blue fill light to preserve readable shadow-side hull detail.
- Performance-conscious geometry and materials suitable for Godot Forward+.
- Texture budget: generally 1K for small/secondary assets and up to 2K for hero ships and major environment surfaces. Do not author 4K textures for the first playable release.
- Capture and review gameplay at 1920×1080. Target stable 60 FPS on a reasonable mid-range Windows gaming PC; profile before increasing geometry, texture size or post-processing.
- Prefer silhouette, layered geometry, material breakup, decals, lighting and restrained effects over oversized textures or excessive mesh density.

This remains a target, not a statement that the current demo already meets the target.

## Fleet roster

All seven ship classes have editable Blender source files, Godot-ready GLBs and preview renders under `art_source/ships`, `assets/models/ships` and `assets/previews`:

1. Player Fighter Mk.I — starter player craft.
2. Raider Interceptor — hostile interceptor; purchasable and pilotable.
3. Aegis Heavy Fighter — heavy assault craft; purchasable and pilotable.
4. Bastion Bomber — strike/ordnance craft; purchasable and pilotable.
5. Mule Cargo Hauler — display-only logistics hull.
6. Vanguard Frigate — display-only escort hull.
7. Leviathan Cruiser — display-only capital hull.

The four small fighter-class ships can be selected in the hangar. The other three remain display-only until their flight handling, collision envelopes, weapons and mission roles are implemented.

## Production and runtime

- Blender 4.5.16 production run generated all seven `.blend`, `.glb` and studio preview outputs; asset validation passed 7/7.
- Godot 4.7.2 imported and parsed all seven generated GLBs; Windows export and executable smoke test passed on the production branch.
- Editable `.blend` files are stored under `art_source/ships/`, protected from Godot resource import by `art_source/.gdignore`.
- Player Fighter Mk.I is generated by `scripts/blender/build_player_fighter.py`.
- Raider Interceptor is generated by `scripts/blender/build_enemy_interceptor.py`.
- Fleet expansion assets are generated by `scripts/blender/build_fleet_expansion.py`.
- Runtime GLB loading uses Godot's `GLTFDocument` / `GLTFState` path to avoid dependence on editor-generated import cache state.
- The selected pilot ship's GLB and presentation scale are loaded into the player flight scene from the persistent profile.
- The showroom loads each GLB independently and rotates it for inspection.
- Full HD visual QA captures eight deterministic gameplay states and the seven-ship showroom. The latest visual capture run is linked from the [README](../README.md).

## Acceptance gate for final art

1. Inspect front, side, top, rear-three-quarter and close-up views for every ship.
2. Confirm no overlapping/misplaced parts, floating details, broken normals, transparent-material artifacts or clipped silhouettes.
3. Confirm player and Raider silhouettes are immediately distinguishable without relying on color.
4. Validate scale, orientation, material preservation and static-mesh stability in Godot 4.7.2.
5. Capture each ship in the hangar and at gameplay chase-camera distance; it must remain a focal object.
6. Tune exposure, key/rim/fill lighting, bloom and engine emissives so armor detail remains visible.
7. Run Godot import, regression and Windows export smoke tests after asset changes.
8. Complete a human interactive Windows playtest. Headless CI and screenshots do not replace this step.

## Known visual work remaining

- The first full Blender production pass now generates all seven classes and passes geometry, material, bounds, preview, GLB and Godot import validation. The preview renders are ready for human art review.
- The generated models are still procedural hard-surface assets, not final AAA models. Heavy Fighter, Frigate and Cruiser need stronger silhouette differentiation; all classes need further close-up art review, UV/texturing work, damage states, animation, lighting and effects refinement.
- The demo's Full HD captures are deterministic QA output, not a substitute for a human art review.
- 4K textures and 4K rendering are explicitly out of scope for the first playable release.
